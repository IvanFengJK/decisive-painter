What it is

Decisive Painter is a small painting toy. Decisions, not diffusion: a painter that picks from menus. It is built around an art-director harness. For every mark, a director answers a few small typed questions (which mark, where, how big, with what medium, which colour of the palette, how to fill it, how heavy) by reading a short text description of the painting so far, including a 5×5 grid of words read back from the real pixels. A seeded renderer then draws the mark. You can open "What the director sees" on the main page to read exactly that text and the answers.

There is no AI model inside this page. The live director is a set of rules and hand-written recipes: about sixty scene types with their keywords, plus everyday words for colours, weather, time of day, seasons, settings, moods and broad subjects. Its per-mark preferences are tables we wrote, combined with average choices measured from an open model's recorded paintings (see Licences).

The pictures are abstract, scene-like paintings: bands, discs, mounds, washes and lines arranged in a composition and a palette that suit the words. They are not pictures of the thing you typed.

The director is a swappable interface. A browser model, or your own server, can answer the same questions without changing the page or the renderer. Under "Advanced" you can point the page at an endpoint you run yourself; that is off by default, and the page warns you that your text would then be sent to it.

Same scene, seed, number of marks, skin and version always give the same painting, on any device. Share links carry only the look, the seed, the number of marks and the skin, never your words or an image.

Decisions, not diffusion

A typed-question decision model reads a short text and answers questions of three kinds: choice (one option from a list), yes/no, and score (a level on a scale). For each question it gives a probability for every option, all from one pass over the text. Code then picks an option from those odds and does the drawing.

This page uses that shape because every decision is small, named and checkable: the odds panel under the painting shows the probabilities for every question of every mark, and marks the option that was picked.

A diffusion model works differently: it generates the pixels of an image. Here no pixels are generated by a model. Every mark is a recorded choice from fixed menus, drawn by a seeded renderer, so any painting can be replayed exactly from its choices. That is what share links and Replay do.

The model slot is empty today: a rules table makes the choices. The Director interface is where a model plugs in, and the badge above the painting names whatever is deciding.

What happens to what you type

  • It stays in this page. The page makes no network requests while painting: no analytics, no cookies, no fonts or scripts from other sites.
  • Every text first goes through input guardrails:
    • clean-up: at most 80 characters, invisible and direction-changing characters removed, code and markup refused;
    • a hand-written filter for sexual content, any sexual content involving minors, graphic violence or gore, hate and slurs, self-harm, and targeting a named private person. It sees through letter swaps, spacing tricks, lookalike letters and repeated letters, and it lets ordinary scene words through (a blood orange sunset, a shooting star, a knife and fork on a table);
    • if a text is refused, the page shows one neutral sentence, paints nothing, logs nothing and sends nothing;
    • a slot for an extra guard model, which may only add refusals and is switched off;
    • your words appear in one clearly marked field of the director's text, and every answer must come from fixed lists, so instructions typed into the box are just words.
  • "Save" keeps up to 20 paintings in this browser only (recipe and a small thumbnail). Nothing is uploaded.
